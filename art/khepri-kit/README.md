# Khepri terrain kit

Runtime file: `public/assets/khepri/khepri-kit.glb` (663,388 bytes, sha256 `bd9efc2ca4bc8951ab6160a08584c739362a94f1bfe711cbcf1864ec194dff53`).
Contract: [docs/13-khepri-world.md section 3](../../docs/13-khepri-world.md).

## Provenance

- All geometry, vertex colours and UVs are original and generated by code in `tools/blender/khepri_kit.py`. No geometry was modelled by hand, imported, or taken from third-party assets. The GLB contains no textures or images.
- Built with Blender 5.2.2 LTS (hash `d13f752e3b9c`, bundled Python 3.13) and its bundled glTF exporter (`io_scene_gltf2` 5.2.40), headless on the Windows host. Generated 14 October 2026. Phase A (14 October) added spires, walls, fallen columns and boulders, then the alien landmarks: crystal spires, a rosette and hoodoos. Each addition left every earlier node unchanged; their decoded attributes, indices and materials are identical to the previous build.
- Determinism: a fixed seed string (`KIT_SEED`), per-piece `random.Random` streams derived from the piece name, and no time-based values. Three consecutive builds produced byte-identical GLBs, matching the hash above.
- `preview-diorama-textured.png` alone also uses the CC0 Poly Haven maps in `art/source/polyhaven/` (`rock_face_03` on basalt, `dry_ground_rocks` on the preview ground). They are applied only to the preview materials after export; see that folder's README for their provenance.

cd /mnt/c && "/mnt/c/Program Files/Blender Foundation/Blender 5.2/blender.exe" --background --factory-startup \

### What each piece is

- **Clumps:** 5–9 power-diagram (weighted Voronoi) columns per clump with 5–7 sides. Neighbours share near-parallel faces separated by 12–26 mm crevices. Tops snap to two or three terrace levels so neighbouring tops merge into flat mesa blocks with a broken, stepped edge. Each clump has an 18 mm top chamfer, a few tilted or broken-stub tops, and occasional horizontal joints with thin dusty ledges. Profiles:

  - 0: a single hoodoo
  - 1: a tall hoodoo with a small capped sibling

## Runtime conventions

- **Axes and seating:** +Y up. Every piece extends 0.12 (crystals 0.13–0.15) below y = 0. Clumps, columns, spires, walls, crystal spires, the rosette and hoodoos have their highest vertex at exactly y = 1.0, intended for runtime y-scaling to formation height. Fallen columns and boulders are authored at real size; do not y-scale them.
- **Hard edges:** all faces are flat-shaded, so NORMAL holds per-face normals and vertices are split per face.
- **Materials (exactly two):**
  - `khepri_basalt`: baseColorFactor 1 (default), roughness 0.92, metallic 0, single-sided. Albedo comes entirely from COLOR_0.
  - `khepri_mineral`: baseColor #2fe0d4, emissiveFactor #2fe0d4 (linear 0.028, 0.745, 0.658, core glTF with no emissive-strength extension), roughness 0.6, single-sided. Under ACES tone mapping it renders as a pale turquoise (about #87dbd6). For the exact saturated #2fe0d4, render it with `toneMapped = false` or add a glow sprite.
- **Mineral faces:** only `basalt_clump_1`, `basalt_clump_4` and `basalt_wall_1` have them. The wall has one crack plus one crust band. The crystal spires are mostly mineral, with a basalt base.
  - Two widened (50 mm) cracks per clump whose facing walls are mineral for their full height. They read as glowing seams between tops and at the crack mouths.
  - A jagged band (0.10–0.30 high) at the foot of two exterior columns, glowing on three contiguous faces plus the crack faces.
- **COLOR_0:** VEC4, normalized unsigned short, **linear** values (glTF convention; three.js GLTFLoader treats it as linear), alpha 1.
  - Basalt: dusty ochre on upward faces and ledges (one dust tone per piece, ±12% per column). Dark warm basalt sides with per-face vertical streaking. Darker base and crevices from baked ray-cast AO (64 rays, 0.42 radius, ground plane included).
  - Mineral faces: white, so the material colour is unchanged.
  - Crystal shards: grey 0.55 at the buried base to 1.0 at the tip.
- **TEXCOORD_0:** a world-scale box projection, 1 UV unit = 1 unit, with no normalisation or packing; intended for `RepeatWrapping`. The axis is chosen per face by the dominant normal component (ties go to Y, then X, then Z), in object-local glTF coordinates:
  - `|nY|` dominant (tops, ledges): `(u, v) = (X, Z)`
  - `|nX|` dominant: `(u, v) = (−sign(nX)·Z, −Y)`
  - `|nZ|` dominant: `(u, v) = (sign(nZ)·X, −Y)`

  Wall textures are upright (v grows downward, the glTF convention) and not mirrored. Seams follow face boundaries, which the flat shading already splits. UVs are object-space: when the runtime scales a clump in y by 1.2–2.2, wall textures stretch vertically by the same factor. Scale the texture repeat or use world-space triplanar mapping if that is unwanted. The build checks every exported UV against this formula from POSITION and NORMAL.

### Gaps against the concept art

- **Scale:** kit pieces are deliberately small next to the relief. They read as outcrops, which sells the miniature-geography scale the user asked for. The terrain itself has to supply the large cliff faces.
- **No bloom:** without bloom, mineral accents are pale cores without the concept's cyan halo.
- **Pool surfaces:** the flat mineral discs are a stand-in. The runtime is expected to draw its own pools and ground glow.
